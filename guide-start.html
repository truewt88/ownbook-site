Start here

Your first fifteen minutes, and your first week

The app teaches you as you go. This is for when you'd rather read first. Follow along with the app open, on the computer or the phone: they work the same way, and neither needs the other.

The 15-minute start

  1. Open the app and choose Start fresh. On the computer it's the first card on the welcome screen; on the phone, tap Start a new book. Pick the currency Your money is in.
  2. Say what you have today. Each bank account you spend from, with its balance today, and each card, with what you owe on it today. Roughly right is fine; you'll check it against your bank later. Savings, investments, CPF and loans can wait.
  3. See your starting money. Ready to place is your bank money less what the cards owe, because that part is already spoken for. If the cards owe more than the bank holds, you start at $0: that's fine, and the rest stays on the cards to pay off.
  4. Choose where to start. From Today is quickest. With Past Statements Too brings in older bank files first, so Reports shows your past spending. Either way, older months never change what you have to place.
  5. Place your first dollars. Budget opens with Your First Month, a short list that ticks itself as you go. Pick a category (on the phone, tap its group, then the category) and give it an amount. Start with what you know: rent, food, bills.
Placing is only a plan.It gives your money a job and moves nothing in your bank. Change it whenever you like, and you don't have to place it all today.

Your book is kept in a folder on your computer, or on the phone if you started there. To keep a copy safe, or to use the phone and the computer together, put it in a Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive folder: on the computer, Settings › Data Folder; on the phone, Settings › Sync › Keep a copy in a folder.

Your first week

Add your spending

  • By hand, as you go: on the computer, Transactions; on the phone, the round Add button, then Spending or income. A few seconds each, and it's the surest way to notice where money goes.
  • From a statement: download a CSV from your bank, then Import on Transactions (on the phone, Add › Import a statement, or share the file to the app). Check the rows before you add them: the dates, money in and out the right way round, the account, and the total against your statement.

How cards work

A card purchase is spending the day you make it, from the category you pick. Paying the card's bill later isn't spending again: it moves money from your bank to the card. Record the bill as a transfer to the card, not as a new expense. Your card then shows less owed, and nothing is counted twice.

Check it matches your bank

A week in, open your banking app and compare. The first-month list has a Count my eggs button, and every bank and card account has one on Nest › Accounts: type what your bank shows, and the app says whether it matches. If it doesn't, it shows what might explain the gap (usually a payment not added yet), and for a small gap you can't trace it can set the balance with a Balance adjustment. Once these agree, you can trust every figure the app shows you.

Plan for something bigger

A trip, yearly insurance, a new phone: costs you can see coming. Give each one a category and place a little every month, so the big month isn't a bad month. Ways to handle… walks through the usual cases.

When the month ends

On the first day of the next month, Budget shows Your First Month Is Done: what you spent, what you placed, and how many categories ran out. There are no right or wrong figures; it's just what happened. On the computer, Placed vs Spent shows each month, category by category. What you didn't spend stays in each category, so you don't place it again; Ready to place shows only new money with no job yet.

In a new book, a short tip appears the first time something happens, like a category running over, a card bill or a big one-off spend. Each shows once, and × hides it for good. Settings › Getting Started brings back the first-month list, or the tips you've hidden.
