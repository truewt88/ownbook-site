Philosophy

Money is personal. The software should be too.

A few beliefs shape every decision in Ownbook: what it does, what it refuses to do, and how it's paid for.

Why it exists

Ownbook started as one person's tool. The budgeting apps worth using had moved to subscriptions, wanted a login to every bank, and kept your financial life on their servers. The spreadsheets that replaced them got slower and more fragile every year.

So this was built instead: a proper budgeting app that runs on your own computer, reads the statements your bank already gives you, and keeps everything in a folder you control. It's now shared in the hope it's useful to you too.

Your data is yours

Your transactions, balances and plans live in one folder: a database file and a few small settings files. There's no account to create, no server of ours to trust, and nothing to lose if this project ever stops. You can move the folder, back it up, sync it through your own Google Drive or Dropbox, or delete it.

The app never asks for your bank password. You bring in the CSV statements your bank provides, and nothing else gets near your accounts.

Budget the money you have

A budget built on next month's paycheque is a wish. Ownbook only lets you plan money that has actually arrived: you assign it to categories until none is left unplanned, and when something runs over, you decide where the money comes from. Over time you end up spending last month's income instead of waiting for the next one.

Most of us didn't need more charts. We needed to see each month clearly and make a decision.

Count the eggs you have, not the ones you hope for. That's the whole idea behind the Budget: money that's arrived waits until you give it a place.

Numbers that add up

Every figure should be one you could check by hand. Overspending comes out of something visible. Investment accounts are valued at holdings plus cash, the same number your broker shows. When something doesn't add up, a missing trade or a typo, the app points at the exact row instead of quietly rounding it away.

The checks are plain rules written into the app, not guesses. The same data always gives the same answer.

Calm, not clever

No streaks, badges or notifications designed to bring you back. No ads, no upsells, no "insights" written to make you feel bad. The app should be quiet, fast and readable, something you open for five minutes, understand, and close.

Value for value

Ownbook is free to download and use for everyday budgeting: placing money, transactions, importing statements, accounts, your Nest and the monthly report. No ads, and your data is never locked: you can always open, export and back it up.

A few advanced tools are Ownbook Pro: planning (when you could stop working, how well the plan holds up, paying off debts, where your CPF is heading), reports over any date range, placed vs spent, long-range goals, investment returns, the cost basis audit, broker imports and the Charcoal dark look on the computer. Each one is clearly marked, and you can try them all free for 14 days.

If it gives you something of value, more clarity or less stress or one good decision, you're invited to give back what it's worth to you. Money keeps the work going, but so does time and talent: reporting a bug, suggesting a bank to support, or telling a friend.

This only works if it's genuinely voluntary, so it will stay that way. Ways to give back.
